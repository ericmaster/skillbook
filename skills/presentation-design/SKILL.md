# Presentation design

Make the audience see the idea, not read the document. Adapt the visual language
to the project's brand and approved examples; preserve effective existing slides.

## 1. Establish the brief and source

Identify audience, decision sought, duration, projection format, brand assets and
authoritative content/generator. Separate facts, hypotheses, targets and proposed
activities. Keep commercial conditions and safety/privacy qualifications intact.
Resolve uncertainty about meaning before choosing a visual.

Audit the existing deck as a sequence, especially its final third. For each slide,
state the one sentence the audience should remember and why the next slide follows.
An existing approved example establishes taste, not a mandatory layout.

Done: one audience, one primary ask, and one intended takeaway per slide.

## 2. Choose the visual argument

Choose a composition because it explains the relationship:

| Meaning | Useful visual | Decision check |
|---|---|---|
| A lived problem | Human scene, object or short illustrative dialogue | Does it feel recognizable without implying a real testimonial? |
| Scale or evidence | One dominant number with denominator, period and source | Is it observed evidence rather than a decorative statistic? |
| Change or mechanism | Before/after, bridge, path or converging routes | Do arrows express a proposed mechanism, sequence or measured change accurately? |
| Alternatives | Shared comparison criteria or complementary roles | Is the comparison fair, without inventing competitor limitations? |
| Pilot or adoption | Timeline with decision gate, inputs and next step | Can the audience understand what happens and when commitment begins? |
| Evidence versus unknown | Anchored proof on one side, clearly marked frontier on the other | Are implemented features distinct from adoption, revenue and impact? |
| A decision or ask | Large direct question, one action and a human/contact anchor | Can the listener act without choosing between unrelated requests? |

Use literal diagrams for exact relationships and editorial illustration for human
meaning. Label illustrative scenes and hypothetical outcomes. Choose charts only
when real values warrant them. Prefer original brand assets, real screenshots and
simple vectors over generic decoration. Keep shape, arrow and color semantics consistent.

Done: one dominant visual argument, supported by only the text it needs.

## 3. Compose rhythm and alternating contrast

Storyboard the deck before polishing individual slides. Alternate selected axes:
human scene ↔ evidence, wide context ↔ close detail, large number ↔ short phrase,
open composition ↔ structured diagram, left emphasis ↔ right emphasis, light ↔
localized dark accent. Change at a narrative turn; avoid mechanical odd/even styling.
Preserve recurring typography, spacing, logo treatment and accent colors.

**Dark-area budget:** use a light canvas with roughly **30% dark area** on accented
slides; **60% is the maximum** unless the user explicitly changes this constraint.
Measure the combined dark surface, including photographs and overlays—not only
CSS panels. Keep dark accents optional: contrast can come from scale and whitespace.
Use a side band, inset panel or bottom strip; place the key tension or decision there.
Check existing full-bleed dark slides against the same budget when applying a redesign.

Alternate at least two useful visual axes across a run of similar slides rather
than repeating a grid with different headings. Save the strongest quiet composition
for the ask. Let the last slides resolve the opening story instead of becoming an appendix.

## 4. Edit for projection

Write a short assertion or question as the headline. Keep explanations in speaker
notes or linked support, while retaining visible qualifications needed to interpret
numbers, prices, evidence and promises. Use the existing generator and content
source so regeneration preserves the design.

At a 1200×675 reference canvas, aim for 44–64px headlines and 22–28px core copy.
Treat these as starting points, not an excuse to squeeze content. Distinguish
readable source/support text from the spoken message. Reduce text or split ideas
before shrinking meaningful copy. Use generous margins and intentional alignment.
Use WCAG contrast checks as a floor: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text;
projection usually needs more. Place text on a solid surface if a photo is variable.

## 5. Validate every slide and the delivery

Render the actual presentation, with fonts and images loaded. Capture **every slide**
and a contact sheet; inspect both full-size views and the sequence. Automated
geometry checks are necessary but cannot judge whether the idea projects clearly.

For each slide verify:
- The intended takeaway is apparent within a few seconds; the title and visual agree.
- Meaningful text is readable at presentation size; no awkward wraps, clipping,
  overlaps, hidden labels, footer collisions or unintended overflow.
- Images have loaded, faces/product controls are sensibly cropped, logo variants
  match their backgrounds, and dark-area budget/contrast are respected.
- Evidence, assumptions and proposed results remain distinguishable; no new promises.
- Keyboard navigation, deep links and any intended reveals function. A static PDF
  contains the complete intended message, without relying on animation or hover.

Export the PDF, assert page count/order/dimensions and inspect rendered pages—not
only the browser version. Confirm selectable text and correct fonts where supported.
Test the smallest intended projector/desktop viewport. Test mobile only if part of
the brief; a fixed-ratio projector deck need not become a vertically stacked website.
Regenerate and check for drift. Keep downloadable formats and speaker notes consistent;
identify raster-only exports honestly instead of calling them editable.

Done: every slide has a recorded visual review; browser and PDF checks pass, or
specific unresolved failures are disclosed. Deliver the usable file and concise
changes. Keep screenshots/test receipts in the project's approved evidence store.
